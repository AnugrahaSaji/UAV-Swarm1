\subsection{Signature Primitive Evaluation}
\label{sec:sig}

Table~\ref{tab:sig_full} presents the complete benchmark of eight
post-quantum signature schemes spanning three NIST security levels,
measured on RPi4 at 1.8\,GHz with 100~iterations and INA219 power
instrumentation.  The results expose a three-regime landscape with
radically different UAV suitability profiles.

\textbf{Lattice-based schemes (ML-DSA, Falcon)} dominate the
real-time regime.  ML-DSA stays below 2\,ms across all three
operations at every security level, with keygen completing in
under 0.75\,ms---500$\times$ faster than SPHINCS$^+$.  However,
rejection sampling induces sign CoV of 49--65\%, producing a wide
latency tail (P95 $\approx$ 2.6--3.9\,ms) that must be budgeted in
hard-deadline control loops.  Falcon-512 achieves the fastest
signing at 0.78\,ms \emph{and} the smallest signature at 654\,B,
making it the Pareto-dominant choice at Level~1.  Falcon keygen is
50$\times$ slower (19.3\,ms) but amortized over key lifetime, and
its deterministic signing yields CoV $<$4\%---ideal for
predictable timing guarantees.

\textbf{SPHINCS$^+$} signs 1\,000$\times$ slower (1.47--2.61\,s)
with per-signature energy costs of 6.0--10.6\,J, approaching the
rekey interval at short periods and consuming orders of magnitude
more energy than a MAVLink control cycle.  Yet verification remains
fast ($\leq$3.3\,ms) and its hash-based construction avoids
lattice-specific structural assumptions---a relevant consideration
for long-lived certificates rather than per-session signing.
SPHINCS$^+$ has the smallest keys (32--64\,B PK) but the largest
signatures (7.9--29.8\,KB), inverting the bandwidth trade-off.

Fig.~\ref{fig:sig_three_panel} decomposes latency by operation.
Keygen spans four orders of magnitude: ML-DSA generates keys in
0.38--0.75\,ms while SPHINCS$^+$ requires 187--283\,ms and Falcon
sits between at 19--54\,ms (high variance, $\sigma = 26$--29\% of
mean, from NTTR sampling).  The sign panel reveals the critical
three-regime split: Falcon and ML-DSA cluster below 2\,ms while
SPHINCS$^+$ occupies the 1.5--2.6\,s range---a 1\,885$\times$ gap
between Falcon-512 and SPHINCS$^+$-128s.  Verification is
universally fast: all eight algorithms verify in $<$3.3\,ms,
confirming that the GCS-side verification path is never the
bottleneck.

Fig.~\ref{fig:sig_dual_axis} overlays sign latency with signature
size, exposing Falcon-512's Pareto dominance: it simultaneously
minimizes both metrics.  ML-DSA signatures grow from 2.4\,KB to
4.6\,KB across levels while remaining sub-2\,ms,
whereas SPHINCS$^+$ signatures balloon to 7.9--29.8\,KB.
For bandwidth-constrained UAV links, Falcon-512's 654\,B signature
fits within a single MAVLink v2 frame without fragmentation.

Fig.~\ref{fig:sig_sizes_latency} juxtaposes artifact sizes against
operational latency.  The left panel reveals SPHINCS$^+$'s inverted
size profile: PK and SK bars are barely visible (32--128\,B) while
signature bars tower at 7.9--29.8\,KB.  ML-DSA maintains balanced
KB-scale artifacts across all three quantities.  Falcon achieves
the smallest signatures (654--1\,267\,B) with moderate key sizes.
The right panel confirms the timing three-regime split visible
from Table~\ref{tab:sig_full}: sign latency spans three orders of
magnitude while verify clusters tightly below 3.3\,ms.

\textbf{UAV selection guidance.}  For real-time C2 authentication,
Falcon-512 ($\star\star\star$) is the optimal choice: 0.97\,ms
combined sign+verify, 654\,B signatures, 3.5\,mJ per handshake,
and deterministic timing (CoV~3\%).  Its 19\,ms keygen is acceptable
given pre-mission key provisioning.  ML-DSA provides the
conservative general-purpose default across all levels, with
sub-2.6\,ms combined cost but wider latency tails requiring 4\,ms
worst-case budgets.  SPHINCS$^+$ is categorically unsuitable for
per-session signing but remains viable for long-lived root
certificates where its hash-based security assumptions offer
maximal cryptographic diversity.
